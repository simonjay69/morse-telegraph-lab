# 📡 CW Spark — Morse Telegraph Trainer & Signal Lab

An immersive, browser-native workshop for anyone who wants to *feel* Morse code rather than merely memorize it. CW Spark recreates the tactile rhythm of an electrical telegraph key, then blends that old-world charm with a modern training lab: adaptive lessons, live decoding, and a global operator community.

This project is a spiritual successor to classic telegraph simulators, but it reimagines the practice bench as a place where beginners, contest operators, and curious hobbyists can forge their own path through dots and dashes.

## 🌱 Why CW Spark Exists

Long before text messages, before emojis, before the internet itself, the telegraph key was the world's fastest way to send a thought across a continent. Morse code is not just a relic — it is a rhythm, a language of patience, and a surprisingly modern cognitive workout.

CW Spark was born from a simple frustration: most Morse trainers treat learning as a flashcard drill. They show you a letter, they play a beep, and they move on. That approach works for memorization, but it ignores the *music* of Morse code — the cadence, the spacing, the swing of an experienced operator's fist.

So we built a different kind of trainer. One that lets you press an actual key (or tap your spacebar, or your touchscreen), feel the timing, and slowly develop what old-timers call a "good fist." The goal is not just recognition. The goal is fluency.

## 🧠 The Training Philosophy

Most people learn Morse code in one of two ways. The first is the "lookup table" method: memorize which pattern maps to which letter. The second is the "sound association" method: train your ear to hear a rhythm and let your brain do the mapping automatically.

CW Spark leans hard into the second approach, because that is how real operators actually operate. When you hear a familiar rhythm, you do not translate it. You simply *know* it, the same way you know the sound of your own name.

The app structures practice around three pillars:

1. **Exposure** — short, frequent bursts of correctly-spaced code.
2. **Pattern recognition** — identifying whole characters and common word fragments by sound.
3. **Live sending** — developing muscle memory by transmitting your own code.

Each pillar is reinforced with immediate feedback, gentle difficulty scaling, and encouragement that respects your time.

## 🎛️ Adaptive Curriculum Engine

The heart of CW Spark is a curriculum engine that watches how you learn. It tracks which characters trip you up, which speeds feel comfortable, and which session lengths keep you engaged. Then it builds tomorrow's lesson accordingly.

If you consistently nail the letter E but stumble on Q, the engine will quietly slip more Q into your practice sets. If your accuracy drops when speed rises above 18 WPM, it will hold you at a comfortable plateau until you are ready to climb.

There is no single "correct" path through the app. There is only your path.

## 🕹️ Telegraph Key Simulation

The virtual key is modeled after classic straight keys and iambic paddles. You can adjust:

- Spring tension (how much force is needed to close the contact)
- Contact travel (how far the lever moves)
- Damping (how quickly the lever returns)
- Sidetone delay (the tiny lag between press and beep)

These details matter. A stiff key teaches precision. A loose key teaches speed. Playing with the settings is half the fun.

## 🔬 Signal Decoding Laboratory

The decoding lab throws you into realistic conditions. You will hear signals buried in static, fading, and QRM (interference from other stations). You will learn to pick out a callsign from a pileup, copy a weak signal at the edge of readability, and recognize common abbreviations used on the air.

This is where classroom knowledge becomes field skill.

## 🔊 Audio Synthesis & Tone Shaping

A pure sine wave is clean but boring. CW Spark uses a Web Audio synthesis engine that lets you shape your sidetone with:

- Waveform selection (sine, triangle, square, and custom harmonic profiles)
- Rise and fall envelopes to avoid key clicks
- Optional bandpass filtering to simulate receiver characteristics
- Pink noise beds for realistic copy practice

The result is a listening experience that feels alive rather than synthetic.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a physical telegraph key?**
No. You can practice entirely with your keyboard, mouse, or touchscreen. But if you *do* own a key, CW Spark supports USB and serial interfaces.

**Is this only for licensed amateur radio operators?**
Not at all. Many users are hobbyists, puzzle enthusiasts, historians, or people who simply enjoy learning unusual skills.

**Can I use CW Spark on a slow connection?**
Yes. Once the app is cached, it runs entirely offline.

**Will my progress be saved?**
Yes, locally by default. Optional cloud sync is available if you want to move between devices.

**Is there a paid tier?**
CW Spark is open source and community-driven. Any future premium features would be clearly marked and never interfere with core learning.
